\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Pixel scale}
Of the \nConfigs{} camera configurations listed in Table~\ref{tab:configs}, \nConfigsOwn{} have enough discharges and spread in $R_{\rm LCFS}$ for individual fitting. These six configurations cover \nShotsOwnScale{} of the \nShotsProcessed{} discharges processed. For the other configurations, we obtained a pooled slope fit with all discharges of that campaign (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). In all of these fits, the limb peak column falls linearly with $R_{\rm LCFS}$, with Spearman correlations between \scaleOwnRhoWeak{} and \scaleOwnRhoStrong. For all slopes, the sign is consistent with the SOL direction seen on the images. The fitted scale ranges from \scaleOwnMin~mm\,pixel$^{-1}$ to \scaleOwnMax~mm\,pixel$^{-1}$. The tightest fit is M6 ``\scaleCfgCampSixView'' with \scaleCfgCampSixN{} discharges, $\rho = \scaleCfgCampSixRho$ and \scaleCfgCampSix~mm\,pixel$^{-1}$ (\scaleCfgCampSixLo--\scaleCfgCampSixHi). The M9 configuration is \scaleCfgCampNine~mm\,pixel$^{-1}$ (\scaleCfgCampNineLo--\scaleCfgCampNineHi).

\citet{farley2019identification} quote a resolution of 5~mm at the tangency plane for wide-angle images, reduced to $256 \times 160$ pixels from the camera's full $640 \times 448$ mode, i.e.\ about 2~mm per native pixel. This is the same order of magnitude as in M9, but 2~mm is outside M9's interval. The archive, however, does not store whether the camera was fitted with the same lens in M9.

We must note two limitations of our calibration. The 10--30\% intervals are large enough to hide bias in specific configurations, e.g.\ if the emission peak is shifted from the LCFS depending on density. The filaments also appear visually similar in pixel units for all configurations: across the \nCfgPx{} fitted configurations with at least 10 discharges each, the median track velocity is \vxPxCfgMin--\vxPxCfgMax{} pixels\,frame$^{-1}$ and width \fwhmPxCfgMin--\fwhmPxCfgMax{} pixels, yet the fitted scales range by a factor of \scaleOwnRatio.

In Table~\ref{tab:campaigns}, the differences between campaigns, which reflect their respective calibrations, are left uninterpreted. A scale error scales both $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ of a configuration by the same factor, so the velocity--size slopes of Section~\ref{sec:analysis} are unaffected, as $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ are normalized to the discharge median values before fitting. The diagonal offset of a discharge in the regime diagram would not affect the conclusions there.

\subsection{Velocity and width distributions}
For these analyses we use tracks from \nShotsStats{} discharges with $\ge 50$ tracks, containing a total of \nTracksSel{} tracks. The pooling of radial velocities and widths across all these discharges yields the distributions in Figure~\ref{fig:distributions}. The median velocity of all discharges is \vMedAll~m/s (95\% CI \vMedAllLo--\vMedAllHi~m/s); the median width is \dMedAll~cm (95\% CI \dMedAllLo--\dMedAllHi~cm). Both distributions have long tails, with velocities out to 2--3~km/s and widths up to 10~cm with a peak at 2--3~cm. The median fraction of tracks moving outward (retained by our near-edge quality cuts) is \outwardMed\%; the inward-moving tracks are concentrated inboard of the limb peak, where streaks move with the plasma rotation. For simplicity, we thus include only the outward-moving tracks in the velocity--size analysis.

\subsection{Independent velocity estimates}
We can estimate the radial velocity without tracking, by correlating fluctuations between pairs of consecutive frames in a near-edge window. We find a median velocity of \xcorrVMed~m/s across the \nXcorr{} discharges with usable correlations, \xcorrRatioMed$\times$ the tracking median (95\% interval \xcorrRatioLo--\xcorrRatioHi). Discharge medians correlate with tracking at Spearman $\rho = \xcorrShotRho$. The sub-unity ratio is expected, since cross-correlation also weights the slow inboard portions of streaks and the stationary limb.

The Lucas--Kanade flow at detection points is another way to estimate velocities. It correlates with track velocities (median per-discharge correlation \lkAgreeMed), and Spearman $\rho = \lkShotRho$ across discharges at the medians. However, it is only \lkRatioMed{} times the track velocity, since the extended, inclined streaks impose the brightness-constancy constraint only normal to them, and any displacement $>1$ pixel/frame along with this constraint biases the flow magnitude low. Thus we use it for the sake of verifying the sign and ordering of velocities only.
