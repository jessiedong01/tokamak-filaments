\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}


\subsection{Pixel scale}
Figure~\ref{fig:calibration} shows the between-shot calibration and Table~\ref{tab:configs} in the appendix lists every camera configuration. Of the \nConfigs{} configurations, \nConfigsOwn{} have enough discharges and enough spread in $R_{\rm LCFS}$ for their own fit, and they cover \nShotsOwnScale{} of the \nShotsProcessed{} processed discharges; the rest use the pooled slope of their campaign. The limb-peak column falls linearly with $R_{\rm LCFS}$ in every fitted configuration, with Spearman correlations between \scaleOwnRhoWeak{} and \scaleOwnRhoStrong{}, and the sign of every slope agrees with the SOL direction found from the images. The fitted scales span \scaleOwnMin{}--\scaleOwnMax{}~mm per pixel. The tightest fit is the M6 configuration ``\scaleCfgCampSixView'' (\scaleCfgCampSixN{} discharges, $\rho = \scaleCfgCampSixRho$), at \scaleCfgCampSix{}~mm per pixel (\scaleCfgCampSixLo--\scaleCfgCampSixHi). The M9 configuration gives \scaleCfgCampNine{}~mm per pixel (\scaleCfgCampNineLo--\scaleCfgCampNineHi). For the same camera, port, and campaign, \citet{farley2019identification} quote a resolution of 5~mm at the tangency plane for wide-angle images reduced to $256 \times 160$ pixels from the camera's $640 \times 448$ mode, which is about 2~mm per native pixel. Our M9 value is of that order, although 2~mm lies below its interval, and the archive does not record whether the lens was the same.

Two features of the calibration limit what the physical units can support. First, the intervals are 10--30\% wide, and a bias common to a configuration, such as a drift of the emission peak relative to the LCFS with density, is not covered by them. Second, in pixel units the filaments look alike in every configuration: across the \nCfgPx{} fitted configurations with at least ten discharges the median track velocity is \vxPxCfgMin{}--\vxPxCfgMax{} pixels per frame and the median width is \fwhmPxCfgMin{}--\fwhmPxCfgMax{} pixels, while the fitted scales differ by a factor of \scaleOwnRatio. The differences between campaigns in Table~\ref{tab:campaigns} are therefore the differences between their calibrations, and we do not interpret them. A scale error multiplies $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ of a configuration by the same factor; it cancels in the velocity--size slopes of Section~\ref{sec:analysis}, which are fitted after normalizing each discharge by its own medians, and it moves a discharge along a diagonal in the regime diagram without changing the conclusions drawn there.

\subsection{Velocity and width distributions}
\nShotsStats{} discharges have at least 50 selected tracks and enter the statistics, with \nTracksSel{} tracks in total. Figure~\ref{fig:distributions} shows the pooled distributions. The median radial velocity over discharges is \vMedAll{}~m/s (95\% interval \vMedAllLo--\vMedAllHi) and the median radial width is \dMedAll{}~cm (\dMedAllLo--\dMedAllHi). The velocity distribution is skewed, with a tail to 2--3~km/s; the width distribution peaks at 2--3~cm and has a tail to 10~cm. Among all near-edge tracks that pass the quality cuts, \outwardMed\% move outward; the inward-moving remainder is concentrated inboard of the limb peak, where the field-aligned streaks sweep across the strip as they rotate with the plasma. We keep only outward-moving tracks in the velocity--size analysis.

\subsection{Independent velocity estimates}
Two estimates that do not use the tracking confirm the velocity scale. The cross-correlation lag between the row-averaged fluctuation profiles of consecutive frames inside the near-edge window, with no detection step, gives a per-discharge median velocity of \xcorrVMed{}~m/s over the \nXcorr{} discharges in which more than half the frame pairs have a usable correlation peak. Its ratio to the tracking median is \xcorrRatioMed{} (\xcorrRatioLo--\xcorrRatioHi), and the two correlate across discharges with Spearman $\rho = \xcorrShotRho$. The lag method weights every bright structure in the window, including the slow inboard part of the streaks and the stationary limb, so a ratio below one is expected. Lucas--Kanade flow at the detections correlates with the track velocities within discharges (median per-discharge correlation \lkAgreeMed; $\rho = \lkShotRho$ between discharge medians) but is smaller by a factor of \lkRatioMed. The streaks are elongated and inclined, so the brightness-constancy constraint fixes only the component of motion normal to the streak, and displacements exceed one pixel per frame; both bias the flow magnitude down. We therefore use the flow only as a sign and ordering check.
