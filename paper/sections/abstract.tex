Filaments are important carriers of cross-field transport in the scrape-off layer of a tokamak. Analytic models predict that their radial velocity varies with their size. We measured the radial velocity and size of a large population of filaments on MAST, using data recorded from a 100~kHz, $48 \times 256$ pixel camera in the outboard midplane in the FAIR-MAST archive. Computer vision techniques were used to automate the identification of filaments and tracking of their trajectories, which yielded \nTracksSel{} filament tracks over \nShotsStats{} discharges in \nCampaigns{} campaigns. The archive does not contain spatial calibration, so we have obtained a pixel scale by regression of the plasma limb column in the image on the EFIT outboard LCFS radius, with bootstrap intervals. The scale is \scaleOwnMin--\scaleOwnMax{} millimetres per pixel.

We find a median velocity of \vMedAll{}~m\,s$^{-1}$, and median radial width of \dMedAll{}~cm. The dependence of velocity on size is mild, with a log--log slope of \vdSlopePooledBlur{} (95\% interval \vdSlopePooledBlurLo{} to \vdSlopePooledBlurHi) when the effects of exposure blurring are corrected for. This is because filaments are not in either velocity regime; they span the range $\hat a = \aHatMin$--$\aHatMax$ in two-region units, which straddles the maximum velocity predicted by the two-region model, but the normalized velocity $\hat v$ is much smaller than the scale of the model, at $\hat v \simeq \vHatMed$.
