\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Filaments are field-aligned structures of increased density and temperature that nucleate near the last closed flux surface (LCFS) in the edge of a tokamak and then move radially outward through the scrape-off layer (SOL). Filaments are responsible for a large part of the cross-field transport of particles and heat \citep{krasheninnikov2001scrape, zweben2007edge, dippolito2011convective}. The radial velocity of filaments determines the distance they travel before being drained to the divertor targets and the load on the first wall. Analytic models predict that the velocity of a filament depends on its size as well as parameters which control how quickly the polarization charge in the filament can be dissipated.

If the charge drains through the target sheath, then the velocity decreases with size as $v \propto a^{-2}$ \citep{krasheninnikov2001scrape}. If the circuit is closed by the ion polarization current instead, the velocity increases with size as $v \propto a^{1/2}$ \citep{garcia2006fluctuations}. These two limits are combined in the two-region model of \citet{myra2006collisionality}, which predicts that the velocity of the filament reaches a maximum at a critical size $a^*$ and falls on either side. The critical size depends on the sound speed, connection length and collisionality.

It is natural to test the validity of these models by measuring the velocities and sizes of a large number of filaments at known plasma conditions. The main diagnostic for this purpose on the Mega Amp Spherical Tokamak (MAST) is a Photron SA1.1 fast camera, which views the outboard midplane in visible light. Most of the analysis to date has used wide-angle views of the outboard midplane, and filaments have been identified either by hand or by software developed to detect filaments in that view. The analysis has been done on a small number of discharges \citep{dudson2008experiments, benayed2009interelm, kirk2016filaments, farley2019identification}, and it has been necessary to fit a model of the camera to get the spatial calibration.

The FAIR-MAST archive \citep{jackson2024fairmast} is focused on making the entire MAST data set public. One recording mode that earlier work has not used at scale is a narrow strip, 48 rows by 256 columns, running through the outboard edge of the plasma, which is recorded at 100{,}000 frames per second.

This strip image is capable of resolving the radial motion of filaments on 10~$\mu$s time intervals. There are only 48 rows in the image, however, which means the image contains information only on radial motion and therefore the radial size of the structures, and no information on other dimensions. The archive does not contain any spatial calibration, so we have to obtain the pixel scale from the data.

We have developed a pipeline to get statistics on the filaments from the raw images, based on standard computer vision techniques: background subtraction using a running minimum model, robust thresholding, detection of connected components, frame-to-frame tracking using the Hungarian algorithm, least-squares fit of tracks and, as an independent estimate of velocity, Lucas--Kanade optical flow. We calibrate the pixel scale against the equilibrium reconstruction (EFIT) by fitting the column in the image where the plasma limb occurs to the radius of the outboard LCFS reported by EFIT, since the limb column moves with this radius from shot to shot. The fit is done at the campaign level with a robust regression, yielding a value for the scale in millimetres per pixel. Bootstrap intervals are obtained for each camera configuration. The calibration uses only the images themselves and the EFIT data. We apply the pipeline to all the usable strip recordings in the FAIR-MAST archive.

The pipeline produces estimates of the radial velocity and radial full width at half maximum for each of the \nTracksSel{} tracks identified in the \nShotsStats{} MAST discharges. The tracks display a median radial velocity of \vMedAll{}~m\,s$^{-1}$ and a median radial FWHM of \dMedAll{}~cm. Eighty-one percent of the tracks are found to be moving outward. The slope of the log--log velocity--size relation is \vdSlopePooled{} before correction for exposure blur and \vdSlopePooledBlur{} for corrected widths, indicating near size-independence of the velocity over the range of observed filaments. In units of the two-region model, the filaments have normalized size of about $\hat a \approx \aHatMin$--$\aHatMax$ and normalized velocity $\hat v \approx \vHatMed$, which is at least an order of magnitude smaller than the characteristic velocity scale of the model.

We use robust regression to fit the dependencies of the median velocity and width on shot-averaged quantities such as plasma current, Greenwald fraction, and beam power, providing confidence intervals on the fitted dependencies. Code and data generated from the analysis are provided with this paper.
