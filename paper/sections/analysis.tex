\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Velocity against size}
Figure~\ref{fig:vd} shows the velocity--size relation after each discharge is normalized by its own medians, and the per-discharge Theil--Sen slope of $\log v_r$ on $\log \delta_r$ has a median of \vdSlopeMed{} (\vdSlopeMedLo--\vdSlopeMedHi) and is positive in \vdSlopePosShare\% of discharges, while the pooled slope is \vdSlopePooled{} (\vdSlopePooledLo--\vdSlopePooledHi) with Spearman $\rho = \vdRhoPooled$. The relation is far from both limiting scalings, since the interval excludes the sheath-connected $\delta^{-2}$ and lies well below the inertial $\delta^{1/2}$.

\subsection{Exposure blur}
A filament that moves during the exposure is smeared along its direction of motion, and because faster filaments are smeared more, the smear produces a positive velocity--width correlation with no physical content. The median smear $v_r t_{\rm exp}$ is \blurMedCm{}~cm, about a tenth of the median width, so the effect is small but measurable, and two tests bound it. In the first test, the slope depends on exposure in the expected direction within M6, where the two common settings share the optics, with a median per-discharge slope of \vdSlopeExpSeven{} (\vdSlopeExpSevenLo--\vdSlopeExpSevenHi) at 7~$\mu$s ($n = \nExpSeven$) and \vdSlopeExpTen{} (\vdSlopeExpTenLo--\vdSlopeExpTenHi) at 10~$\mu$s ($n = \nExpTen$), while the median width stays the same, \dMedExpSeven{} against \dMedExpTen{}~cm, because the extra smear of 3~$\mu$s at 400~m/s is 0.1~cm. The 4~$\mu$s discharges belong to M7 and M8 and cannot be compared in width with those of M6, although their slope, \vdSlopeExpFour{} (\vdSlopeExpFourLo--\vdSlopeExpFourHi), is also low.
In the second test, correcting each width for its own smear in quadrature lowers the pooled slope to \vdSlopePooledBlur{} (\vdSlopePooledBlurLo--\vdSlopePooledBlurHi), and this corrected value is taken as the measurement, which shows that the radial velocity is nearly independent of width over the measured range, with at most a weak positive dependence.

\subsection{Two-region normalization}
With the inputs of Section~\ref{sec:method}, the median reference scales are $a^* = \aStarMed$~cm, $v^* = \vStarMed$~m/s, and $\Lambda = \LambdaMed$, which puts the discharges in the low-collisionality half of the regime diagram, and Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} places each discharge at its median $\hat a = (\delta_r/2)/a^*$ and $\hat v = v_r/v^*$. The filaments span $\hat a = \aHatMin$--$\aHatMax$ (median \aHatMed), a range that straddles the velocity maximum of the model and over whose central half the model $\hat v(\hat a)$ stays within a factor of two of its peak, so a weak net dependence of velocity on size is expected, and this is what the data show. The normalized velocity, however, is only $\hat v \approx \vHatMed$, which means that the measured filaments move at a few percent of the velocity scale of the model. Part of this gap comes from the input assumptions, since $v^* \propto T_e^{7/10}$ and $T_e$ at the LCFS is assumed for \nTeAssumed{} of the \nShotsStats{} discharges, and part from the model itself, which gives the velocity of an isolated, fully formed filament with an order-unity prefactor that simulations in realistic geometry find to be below one \citep{walkden2015characterization, paruta2018blob}. The measurement is also made within 15~cm of the limb, where filaments are still forming and accelerating and may not yet have reached the velocity that the model describes. The ratio is nonetheless larger than the uncertainty in the prefactor alone explains, so the amplitude of the velocity, more than its dependence on size, is the point of tension with the model.

\subsection{Dependence on plasma parameters}
Figure~\ref{fig:params} shows the per-discharge medians against plasma current and Greenwald fraction for the \nShotsL{} non-ELMy discharges, which span $I_p = \ipMin$--\ipMax{}~kA and $f_{\rm GW} = \fgwMin$--\fgwMax, and no dependence on either is resolved. The log--log slope of the median velocity is \slVIp{} (\slVIpLo--\slVIpHi) on plasma current and \slVFgw{} (\slVFgwLo--\slVFgwHi) on Greenwald fraction, the corresponding slopes for the width are \slDIp{} (\slDIpLo--\slDIpHi) and \slDFgw{} (\slDFgwLo--\slDFgwHi), and beam power has no effect on the velocity (\slVNbi, \slVNbiLo--\slVNbiHi) and a small positive effect on the width (\slDNbi, \slDNbiLo--\slDNbiHi). Because these fits pool configurations with different calibrations, they are repeated inside the best-calibrated configuration alone (``\bestCfgView'', \nShotsBestCfgL{} non-ELMy discharges at one scale, $I_p = \ipBestMin$--\ipBestMax{}~kA), where the velocity slopes are \slBVIp{} (\slBVIpLo--\slBVIpHi) on current and \slBVFgw{} (\slBVFgwLo--\slBVFgwHi) on Greenwald fraction, consistent with the pooled result.
\citet{kirk2016filaments} found in a dedicated current scan at fixed density and power that the radial velocity falls with current, whereas in the present sample current, density, heating, and campaign vary together and most discharges sit near 700~kA, so confounding could hide a weak dependence, and the slopes are reported as measured without any claim of contradiction.
