\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Velocity against size}
In Figure~\ref{fig:vd} we plot radial velocity against width for each track, normalized by the median for the discharge in which it was detected. We find a median per-discharge Theil--Sen slope of \vdSlopeMed{} (\vdSlopeMedLo{} to \vdSlopeMedHi), positive in \vdSlopePosShare\% of discharges. The pooled estimate is \vdSlopePooled{} (\vdSlopePooledLo{} to \vdSlopePooledHi) at Spearman $\rho = \vdRhoPooled$. The observed relation departs significantly from the limiting scaling $\propto \delta^{-2}$ for plasma connected through the sheath, and lies well below the inertial scaling $\propto \delta^{1/2}$.

\subsection{Exposure blur}
Filament motion during the exposure time blurs their image in the direction of motion, with greater velocity smearing larger distances. This introduces a spurious positive velocity--width correlation. We find the median smear distance $v_r t_{\rm exp} = \blurMedCm$~cm, only about 1/10th of the median width, so the effect is small but measurable. We thus use two tests to bound this potential bias. First, we observe in M6 a rise of slope with longer exposure time, from \vdSlopeExpSeven{} (\vdSlopeExpSevenLo{} to \vdSlopeExpSevenHi) at 7~$\mu$s ($n = \nExpSeven$), to \vdSlopeExpTen{} (\vdSlopeExpTenLo{} to \vdSlopeExpTenHi) at 10~$\mu$s ($n = \nExpTen$). Median width remains \dMedExpSeven~cm for both exposures, since the additional 0.1~cm smear over the 3~$\mu$s interval, at the median speed of 400~m/s, is negligible.

The 4~$\mu$s exposures lie in M7 and M8, so we cannot compare their widths with M6, and the slope is also low at \vdSlopeExpFour{} (\vdSlopeExpFourLo{} to \vdSlopeExpFourHi). Second, we find that correcting widths for their own smear in quadrature reduces the pooled slope to \vdSlopePooledBlur{} (\vdSlopePooledBlurLo{} to \vdSlopePooledBlurHi), which we take as our measurement. This shows that radial velocity is essentially independent of width over our measured range, with only a possibly slight positive correlation.

\subsection{Two-region normalization}
By applying the inputs for our discharges, as described in Section~\ref{sec:method}, we find the median scales to be $a^* = \aStarMed$~cm, $v^* = \vStarMed$~m/s, and $\Lambda = \LambdaMed$, placing our discharges in the low-collisionality region of the regime diagram. In Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}, we have plotted each discharge's median position on the $\hat a$--$\hat v$ diagram ($\hat a = (\delta_r/2)/a^*$, $\hat v = v_r/v^*$). We find $\hat a$ in the range \aHatMin{} to \aHatMax, with a median of \aHatMed. This range covers the peak of the model's velocity curve (near $\hat a = 1$), and the model's $\hat v(\hat a)$ remains within a factor of two of its peak across the middle half of $\hat a$'s range in the model's Eq.~(\ref{eq:regimes}). We thus expect a weak velocity--size relationship, as confirmed by our data.

However, the normalized velocity is only about \vHatMed, so our filaments move at just a few percent of the model's characteristic velocity. This discrepancy partly reflects our input assumptions, since $v^* \propto T_e^{7/10}$ and we take $T_e$ at the LCFS to be the assumed value for \nTeAssumed{} of our \nShotsStats{} discharges. The model also assumes an isolated, fully formed filament, and includes an order-unity prefactor. This prefactor is found to be below unity in simulations of realistic geometry \citep{walkden2015characterization, paruta2018blob}.

Furthermore, our measurements are made within 15~cm from the limb, where the filaments are still forming and accelerating and may not yet have reached the velocity described by the model. The gap is larger than is expected from the uncertainty in the prefactor alone, so the central disagreement with the model concerns the velocity's magnitude, not its size dependence.

\subsection{Dependence on plasma parameters}
In Figure~\ref{fig:params}, we show the per-discharge medians of velocity and width vs.\ plasma current and Greenwald fraction for our \nShotsL{} non-ELMy discharges. The discharges span a range of \ipMin{} to \ipMax~kA of plasma current and \fgwMin{} to \fgwMax{} of Greenwald fraction. We see no clear dependence on either current or Greenwald fraction. The log--log slopes of our median velocity distributions are \slVIp{} on current (95\% interval \slVIpLo{} to \slVIpHi) and \slVFgw{} on Greenwald fraction (\slVFgwLo{} to \slVFgwHi); those of the median width distributions are \slDIp{} on current (\slDIpLo{} to \slDIpHi) and \slDFgw{} on Greenwald fraction (\slDFgwLo{} to \slDFgwHi). The log--log slopes of median velocity and width on beam power are \slVNbi{} (\slVNbiLo{} to \slVNbiHi) and \slDNbi{} (\slDNbiLo{} to \slDNbiHi), respectively. The fits for the medians of our global sample combine multiple configurations with different calibration settings; a single, best-calibrated configuration, ``\bestCfgView,'' applies to a smaller subset of \nShotsBestCfgL{} non-ELMy discharges, at a single scale setting and plasma currents of \ipBestMin{} to \ipBestMax~kA. When we re-run the fits for this subset, we find velocity slopes of \slBVIp{} (\slBVIpLo{} to \slBVIpHi) on current and \slBVFgw{} (\slBVFgwLo{} to \slBVFgwHi) on Greenwald fraction, in agreement with our global result.

\citet{kirk2016filaments} report a current dependence of radial velocity falling with increasing current, but they study a dedicated current scan at constant density and power. In our sample, the current varies concurrently with density, heating, and research campaign. Moreover, most of our discharges are clustered around 700~kA. Still, a weak dependence might be masked by this confounding. The reported slopes describe our measurements, and are not intended to contradict the earlier result.
