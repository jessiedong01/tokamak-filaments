\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

We derive the pixel scale from the limb peak tracking the radius of the outboard LCFS from the EFIT equilibrium with a fixed offset. This offset may drift with changes to the emission profile which depend on density and neutral pressure, and the EFIT boundary has uncertainty on the order of a centimetre. The bootstrap intervals of Table~\ref{tab:configs} include the scatter of these effects, though we did not account for a potential configuration-wide bias. The filaments appear to have very similar pixel sizes across configurations whose pixel scales vary by almost a factor of two (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Whether this is a coincidence or a sign that some of the spread in scale is not purely optical, we are unable to resolve at present with the archive; none of our conclusions depends on comparing physical units between configurations.

Every value of velocity or width has a systematic uncertainty of 10--30\% from the pixel scale of its configuration, and up to a factor of two uncertainty when comparing between configurations. This uncertainty could be removed by having a per-shot Calcam model, which is the obvious next step if the calibration images are made available. The failure of within-shot regression as a surrogate for Calcam is shown in Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}.

\paragraph{Geometry correction.}
The observed strip is one row band crossing the edge, whose column axis is radial only in the tangency plane. The measured width is the chord length of an inclined streak, which overestimates the perpendicular width by $1/\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the streak and the column axis. We do not correct this because the angle is hard to measure on a 48-row band, and roughly constant over the campaign. This shifts the $\hat a$ parameter but does not affect the velocity--size slope.

\paragraph{Emission blurring.}
The measured emission is integrated along the line of sight. A bright structure in the ``background'', beyond the tangency plane, is projected to a smaller radius, which blurs the SOL distance measurement. This does not affect the velocity because it is a time derivative.

\paragraph{What the camera sees.}
The $D_\alpha$ emission depends on the neutrals as well as plasma density. The neutrals usually fall off sharply inside the LCFS. This means filaments are visible mainly in the SOL. Filament brightness therefore changes as filaments move. Tracking the centroid is robust to the slowly varying amplitude, but optical flow is sensitive to amplitude changes. This is one reason for the low measured flow velocities.

\paragraph{Selection.}
The region of measurement, 10 pixels inboard to 40 pixels outboard, is 3--5~cm inward and 11--21~cm outward (depending on the configuration). This contains filaments at their birth, meaning the velocities in the far SOL may be different. The selection threshold of 50 tracks per discharge keeps the per-discharge medians stable. Tracks near ELMs are ignored. This discards some discharges and some of the largest events. The headline results refer to inter-ELM and L-mode filaments near the edge.

\paragraph{Model inputs.}
The LCFS temperature is assumed for most discharges, and the separatrix density $n_{e,\rm sep}$ is half the line average. Both enter the dimensionless parameters $a^*$, $v^*$, and $\Lambda$ as power laws with exponents less than unity. A factor of 2 uncertainty in either parameter means a discharge moves by less than a factor of 2 in the regime diagram. The conclusion that the sample covers $\hat a = 1$ is robust to these uncertainties. Note that the dimensionless velocity $\hat v$ itself is only known to within a factor of 2.
