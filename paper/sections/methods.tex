\subsection{Strip geometry and the plasma edge}
A recording is an array of $n$ frames of $48 \times 256$ pixels with 10-bit depth. The columns run across the plasma edge: the SOL occupies one end of the strip and the core the other, and which end depends on how the camera was mounted in that campaign. We take the row-averaged brightness profile $p(t, x)$, smooth it over 200 frames (2~ms) in time and $\sigma = 2$ columns in space, and call the darker outer quarter of the columns the SOL side. This fixes a sign $s = \pm 1$ such that outward motion is $s\,\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t$. The edge column $x_{\rm half}(t)$ is the first column, scanning from the SOL side, at which $p$ exceeds the midpoint between the dark level (the mean of the ten outermost SOL-side columns) and the interior level (the median over the columns 40--75\% of the way across the strip from the SOL side), with linear sub-pixel interpolation. The limb peak $x_{\rm peak}(t)$ is the maximum of $p$ within 40 columns inboard of $x_{\rm half}$, refined by parabolic interpolation. Detections are referenced to $x_{\rm half}$ during processing and shifted to $x_{\rm peak}$ at aggregation, because the peak is the feature that the calibration in Section~\ref{sec:calibration} ties to the equilibrium.

\subsection{Background subtraction and detection}
Filaments are transient, so a per-pixel running minimum over 21 frames ($\pm 10$ frames, $\pm 100~\mu$s) estimates the quiescent background \citep{kirk2016filaments}. The fluctuation image is the frame minus this background, passed through a $3 \times 3$ median filter and a Gaussian with $\sigma = 1$ pixel. The noise level $\sigma_n$ is $1.4826$ times the median absolute deviation of all fluctuation pixels in a block of 3{,}000 frames. Pixels above $3\sigma_n$ are labelled into 8-connected components, and components smaller than 12 pixels are dropped. For each remaining component we record the intensity-weighted centroid $(x_c, y_c)$, the peak fluctuation, the orientation and the major and minor axis lengths from the second central moments of the component, and the radial width. The radial width is measured on a chord: we average the fluctuation over the five rows centred on $y_c$ and take the number of columns at or above half the chord maximum. We use a chord rather than the full component because filaments appear as inclined streaks, and the column extent of the whole component grows with the tilt. The signed SOL distance of a detection is $s\,(x_c - x_{\rm half})$.

\subsection{Tracking}
Detections in consecutive frames are linked by the Hungarian method \citep{kuhn1955hungarian} with the Euclidean distance between centroids as the cost and a gate of 12 pixels per frame, which is about 4.5~cm or 4.5~km/s at the calibrated scale. Unmatched detections start new tracks, and a track ends at the first frame without a match. Tracks with fewer than five detections (50~$\mu$s) are discarded. For each track the radial velocity in pixels per frame is the least-squares slope of $s\,x_c$ against frame index, with a standard error from the residuals; the width is the median chord FWHM over the track and the SOL distance is the median signed distance. Recordings are processed in blocks of 3{,}000 frames with a 60-frame overlap. Tracks that start in the overlap are dropped from the earlier block and recovered in the later one.

\subsection{Optical flow as an independent velocity}
As a check on the tracking, we also estimate the velocity at each detection by Lucas--Kanade optical flow \citep{lucas1981iterative}: Sobel spatial gradients and the frame difference in a $7 \times 7$ window around the centroid give a $2 \times 2$ least-squares system for the displacement between frames $f$ and $f + 1$, which we solve when its condition number is below $10^4$. The track-level flow velocity is the mean of the horizontal component over the track's detections, with the sign $s$ applied. The flow estimate uses no assignment step and is sensitive to the brightness profile rather than the centroid, so agreement between the two is evidence that neither is an artefact of its method.

\subsection{ELM detection}
Edge-localized modes (ELMs) eject large filaments that would dominate a velocity distribution. We detect them from the midplane $D_\alpha$ monitor, sampled at 50--250~kHz depending on the campaign: the residual after a 5~ms running median is a spike where it exceeds six times its robust standard deviation, and each spike's time is the centre of mass of its samples. The monitor is coarsely digitized in some campaigns, with only tens of distinct values, which makes the robust standard deviation vanish; the threshold is therefore floored at six digitizer steps and at half the inter-ELM level. Because the $D_\alpha$ channel is absent or saturated in some discharges, we apply the same detector to the frame-mean camera brightness. A track that begins within 1~ms of a spike of either kind is flagged, and discharges with more than five $D_\alpha$ spikes inside the camera window are labelled ELMy. Flagged tracks are excluded from all statistics; the parameter dependences in Section~\ref{sec:results} use the non-ELMy discharges only.

\subsection{Pixel scale from the equilibrium}
\label{sec:calibration}
The archive gives no spatial calibration for the strip recordings, so we recover the scale from the data. The limb peak sits where the line of sight is tangent to the flux surfaces at the LCFS, so its sensor column should move with the outboard LCFS radius $R_{\rm LCFS}$ from EFIT, and the slope of column on radius is the inverse pixel scale. Within a single discharge $R_{\rm LCFS}$ moves by only a few centimetres during the flat top, the EFIT reconstruction is least reliable during ramp-up and termination, and the brightness profile changes shape as the density rises, so a within-shot regression is unstable (Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}). Between discharges, the plasma position varies by 10--20~cm while the camera and its optics stay fixed through a campaign, which gives a much longer lever arm.

For each discharge we take, at every EFIT time inside the flat top, $R_{\rm LCFS}$ as the largest major radius of the EFIT boundary contour within 5~cm of the magnetic-axis height, and $x_{\rm peak}$ from the profile smoothed over 2~ms. EFIT samples whose $R_{\rm LCFS}$ differs by more than 8~cm from the flat-top median are dropped as unconverged. The discharge then contributes one point: its median $x_{\rm peak}$, converted to sensor coordinates by adding the region-of-interest offset recorded in the camera metadata, and its median $R_{\rm LCFS}$. We call a camera configuration the combination of campaign, view string in the camera metadata, and region-of-interest offset, because a change in any of these can mean a change of lens, pointing, or sensor region. A configuration with at least 8 discharges and at least 3~cm of spread in $R_{\rm LCFS}$ gets its own Theil--Sen slope \citep{sen1968estimates}, which we keep if its sign agrees with $s$, the Spearman correlation satisfies $|\rho| \ge 0.5$, and the bootstrap interval excludes zero. Every other configuration uses the pooled slope of its campaign, fitted on all the campaign's configurations after subtracting each configuration's mean $R_{\rm LCFS}$ and mean column, which removes the per-configuration intercepts. The 95\% intervals come from 2{,}000 bootstrap resamples of discharges within configurations \citep{efron1993bootstrap}. The scale is the inverse of the slope magnitude.

\subsection{Physical units and track selection}
A track's radial velocity is $v_r = s\,\dot x_c \times f_{\rm frame} \times \ell$ with $\ell$ the pixel scale, and its radial width is $\delta_r = \mathrm{FWHM} \times \ell$. We measure the SOL distance from $x_{\rm peak}$, using the per-shot median offset $x_{\rm peak} - x_{\rm half}$. A track enters the statistics if its median position lies between 10 pixels inboard and 40 pixels outboard of the limb peak (3--5~cm inboard to 11--21~cm outboard, depending on the configuration's scale), its velocity standard error is below 0.5 pixels per frame, its width is finite, it begins inside the plasma flat top, and it is not flagged as ELM-adjacent. A discharge enters the shot-level statistics if it has at least 50 such tracks with $v_r > 0$.

\subsection{Statistics}
All central values are medians with 95\% bootstrap intervals from 2{,}000 resamples. Slopes are Theil--Sen estimates in $\log_{10}$--$\log_{10}$ coordinates with the estimator's own 95\% interval, and we report Spearman's $\rho$ alongside. The velocity--size slope is computed per discharge and also pooled: for the pooled fit we divide each track's $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ by the medians of its discharge, so that differences between discharges in the pixel scale or the plasma conditions do not create a spurious correlation. To correct for exposure blur we replace $\delta_r$ by $\delta_{\rm corr} = (\delta_r^2 - (v_r t_{\rm exp})^2)^{1/2}$, which treats the smear as a Gaussian of width $v_r t_{\rm exp}$ added in quadrature, and refit. Parameter dependences are Theil--Sen slopes of the per-discharge median $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ on plasma current, Greenwald fraction, and injected beam power, the last offset by 0.05~MW so that Ohmic discharges enter the log fit.

For the two-region normalization we need $T_e$ and $n_e$ at the LCFS, $B$, $q_{95}$, and $R$. $T_e$ comes from the Thomson scattering profile, from the edge system where it exists and the core system otherwise, interpolated at $R_{\rm LCFS}$ and taken as the median over the flat top; when no profile reaches the LCFS we assume $T_e = \teDefault$~eV and say so. We take $n_{e,\rm sep}$ as half the line-averaged density, $B$ as the vacuum toroidal field at the magnetic axis scaled by $R_{\rm axis}/R_{\rm LCFS}$, and $L_\parallel = \pi q_{95} R_{\rm axis}$. The filament radius in the model is half the measured FWHM, $a = \delta_r/2$. $\nu_{ei}$ uses the NRL formulary \citep{nrl2019formulary} with deuterium ions.
