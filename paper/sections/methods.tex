\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The stages of the pipeline for processing one discharge (or part of one) are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:overview}. All stages use the same settings for each shot; no tuning is undertaken on a case-by-case basis.

\subsection{Strip geometry and the plasma edge}
Each recording consists of $n$ frames of size $48 \times 256$ with 10-bit depth. Each column of the strip spans the plasma edge; the SOL and the core are at opposite ends of the strip depending on the mounting of the camera. For each frame we compute the profile of brightness $p(t,x)$ averaged over rows and smoothed in time with a window of 200 frames (2~ms) and spatial $\sigma = 2$ columns. The outer quarter of the columns is darker, and we identify this as the SOL. Thus we define a sign $s = \pm 1$. The velocity is then defined as $s\,\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t$ for outward motion.

We define two important positions $x_{\rm half}(t)$ and $x_{\rm peak}(t)$. The former is the first column from the SOL for which the brightness $p$ exceeds a threshold. The threshold is the midpoint between a ``dark level'' (average brightness of the ten outermost columns on the SOL side) and an ``inside level'' (median brightness of columns between 40\% and 75\% from the SOL side). We locate $x_{\rm half}$ by linear sub-pixel interpolation. $x_{\rm peak}$ is the brightest point within 40 columns from $x_{\rm half}$ on the inside. We perform a parabolic interpolation to refine the latter. We refer detections to $x_{\rm half}$ while processing and shift results to $x_{\rm peak}$ while aggregating. The peak is anchored to the equilibrium by the calibration presented in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.

\subsection{Background subtraction and detection}
As filaments are transient, we estimate the steady background per pixel by the running minimum of the field over 21 frames ($\pm 10$ frames, or $\pm 100~\mu$s) \citep{kirk2016filaments}. The fluctuation is the difference between the frame and the background. We filter the fluctuation with a median filter of size $3 \times 3$ and a Gaussian filter with $\sigma = 1$ pixel. We estimate the noise $\sigma_n$ as 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation of all fluctuation pixels in a set of 3{,}000 frames. We find detection candidates as 8-connected components of pixels above $3\sigma_n$. We discard those with fewer than 12 pixels. We obtain the intensity-weighted centroid $(x_c, y_c)$ of each component as well as its peak fluctuation, its orientation and the major and minor axes of its second central moments. We report the chord FWHM as the width of the detection: the number of columns with fluctuation $\ge$ half the maximum in a 5-row average centred on $y_c$.

We measure width on a chord because of the slanted appearance of filaments. The column width of a component grows with its tilting. We report the signed SOL distance of a detection as $s\,(x_c - x_{\rm half})$.

\subsection{Tracking}
We link detections in successive frames using the Hungarian method \citep{kuhn1955hungarian}, minimizing the distance between centroids. We use a gate of 12 pixels per frame, corresponding to about 4.5~cm or 4.5~km/s at our calibrated scale. New tracks are started when there is no match, and they are ended when there is no match in a frame. We discard tracks with fewer than 5 detections (50~$\mu$s). The radial velocity of a track is calculated as the slope of a least squares linear regression of $s\,x_c$ against frame index with the corresponding standard error from the residuals. The width of a track is calculated as the median of the chord FWHM of its detections, and its SOL distance as the median of the signed distances.

We process recordings in blocks of 3{,}000 frames with an overlap of 60. We discard tracks started in the overlap with the preceding block from the results of the preceding block, but process and include them in the results of the following block.

\subsection{Optical flow as an independent velocity}
We verify our velocity estimate at each detection using Lucas--Kanade optical flow \citep{lucas1981iterative}. Over a $7 \times 7$ window centred on the centroid, we use the two Sobel spatial gradients and the difference between frames as a $2 \times 2$ least squares system for the displacement from frame $f$ to $f+1$. We solve the system whenever it has condition number lower than $10^4$. The flow velocity of a track is the mean of the horizontal components of flow over the track, with sign $s$. This estimate does not involve any assignment, but is responsive to the whole brightness profile in the neighbourhood of the centroid. Agreement between the two estimates is evidence that they are not merely methodological artefacts.

\subsection{ELM detection}
Edge-localized modes (ELMs) eject large filaments that would dominate the velocity distribution, so ELMs are detected from the midplane $D_\alpha$ monitor sampled at 50--250~kHz, depending on the campaign, with a spike defined to be a temporal peak whose residual after subtracting a 5~ms running median exceeds six times its robust standard deviation. The centre of mass in time of each spike's samples is recorded. Some campaigns provided a coarsely digitized version of the $D_\alpha$ monitor, with only tens of discrete values, making its robust standard deviation zero; in these cases the threshold is six digitizer steps, or half the inter-ELM level, whichever is larger.

The $D_\alpha$ channel is missing or saturated in some discharges, so we also apply the detector to the frame means of camera brightness, flagging a track if it starts within 1~ms of any spike. Discharges with more than five spikes in the $D_\alpha$ monitor in the camera time window are labelled as ELMy for the purposes of this paper, and tracks with ELM flags are removed from all statistical analyses. The parameter dependences of Section~\ref{sec:analysis} are all calculated from discharges that are not ELMy.

\subsection{Pixel scale from the equilibrium}
\label{sec:calibration}
The strip recordings in the archive are not spatially calibrated; this scale must be derived from the data. The limb peak position is where the line of sight is tangent to the LCFS, so the EFIT outboard radius of the LCFS, $R_{\rm LCFS}$, should track the sensor column of the peak position. The derivative of the median (over time) column versus $R_{\rm LCFS}$ across discharges gives the inverse scale. Within a discharge, however, $R_{\rm LCFS}$ changes only a few centimetres during the flat top, and EFIT has large error bars during ramp-up and termination. Also, the rising density changes the brightness profile shape, so regression within a shot is unstable (Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}). On the other hand, the plasma moves 10--20~cm between shots within a campaign, while the camera and its optics do not move within a campaign. This gives a long lever arm for a more stable regression.

For every discharge, we record $R_{\rm LCFS}$ at every EFIT time in the flat top. $R_{\rm LCFS}$ is the EFIT boundary contour's maximum major radius within 5~cm of the magnetic axis height, and $x_{\rm peak}$ is taken from the 2~ms smoothed profile. Any EFIT sample with $R_{\rm LCFS}$ more than 8~cm different from the flat-top median is discarded as unconverged. This gives one data point per discharge: the pair of median $x_{\rm peak}$ and $R_{\rm LCFS}$ for the discharge. To convert $x_{\rm peak}$ to sensor coordinates, we simply add the region-of-interest offset from the camera metadata. We define a camera configuration as (campaign, view string, region-of-interest offset), since any change in these three components could mean a different lens, pointing angle, or sensor region.

If a camera configuration has at least 8 discharges with a spread of at least 3~cm in $R_{\rm LCFS}$, we compute its own Theil--Sen slope \citep{sen1968estimates}; otherwise we use a pooled value from its campaign. The configuration slope is kept if it agrees in sign with $s$, has Spearman correlation $|\rho| \ge 0.5$, and its bootstrap 95\% interval excludes 0; otherwise a pooled slope is used instead. To get the pooled campaign slope, we regress column on $R_{\rm LCFS}$ after subtracting each configuration's mean $R_{\rm LCFS}$ and mean column to remove per-configuration intercepts. Bootstrap 95\% intervals are obtained from 2{,}000 bootstrap resamples of discharges within configurations \citep{efron1993bootstrap}. The scale is the inverse of the absolute value of the slope.

\subsection{Physical units and track selection}
Radial velocity is $v_r = s\,\dot x_c \times f_{\rm frame} \times \ell$, and radial width is $\delta_r = \mathrm{FWHM} \times \ell$, with $\ell$ the pixel scale; we measure distance from $x_{\rm peak}$ using the per-shot median offset $x_{\rm peak} - x_{\rm half}$. A track is included in statistics if its median position is within 10 pixels inboard and 40 pixels outboard of the limb peak (3--5~cm inboard and 11--21~cm outboard, depending on configuration scale), its velocity standard error is less than 0.5 pixels/frame, its width is finite, it starts inside the plasma flat top, and it is not ELM flagged. A discharge is included in shot-level statistics if it contains at least 50 tracks with $v_r > 0$, ensuring stable per-shot median calculations.

\subsection{Statistics}
Central values are medians, with 95\% bootstrap intervals from 2{,}000 resamples. Slopes are Theil--Sen estimates in $\log_{10}$ versus $\log_{10}$ space, with 95\% estimator intervals and the Spearman $\rho$ statistic. The velocity--size slope is calculated both for each discharge individually and pooled. For the latter, we normalize each track by dividing $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ by the discharge median values, so as not to introduce an artificial correlation in the pool because of differences between the discharges, like the camera pixel scale (e.g. different camera configurations) or plasma conditions. The exposure blur correction replaces $\delta_r$ by $\delta_{\rm corr} = (\delta_r^2 - (v_r t_{\rm exp})^2)^{1/2}$, then re-fits the slope, treating the smear as a Gaussian with width $v_r t_{\rm exp}$ added in quadrature.

Slopes are Theil--Sen estimates of medians of $v_r$ and $\delta_r$ for each discharge, versus plasma current, Greenwald fraction and injected beam power. The beam power has an offset of 0.05~MW to allow fitting of the Ohmic discharges in the log fit.

The two-region normalization requires $T_e$ and $n_e$ at the LCFS, $B$, $q_{95}$ and $R$. $T_e$ is taken from a Thomson scattering profile, the edge system if available, else the core system. The profile is interpolated at $R_{\rm LCFS}$, and $T_e$ is taken as the median value during the flat top. If no profile is available, $T_e$ is assumed to be \teDefault{}~eV and marked as such. The separatrix density $n_{e,\rm sep}$ is one-half of the line-averaged density. $B$ is the vacuum toroidal field at the magnetic axis, scaled by $R_{\rm axis}/R_{\rm LCFS}$. The connection length is $L_\parallel = \pi q_{95} R_{\rm axis}$. We take the model filament radius as half the measured FWHM, i.e.\ $a = \delta_r/2$. The collision frequency $\nu_{ei}$ is taken from the NRL formulary \citep{nrl2019formulary} with deuterium ions.
