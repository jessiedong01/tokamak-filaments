\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Public archives make it possible to measure filament statistics on hundreds of discharges with a pipeline that requires no tuning by hand, and on MAST the 100~kHz strip recordings give radial velocities and widths for \nTracksSel{} filaments in \nShotsStats{} discharges, with a pixel scale recovered from the equilibrium to 10--30\% for each camera configuration. The radial velocity has a median of \vMedAll{}~m/s and, once exposure blur is removed, depends on filament width with a log--log slope no larger than about 0.1. In two-region units the filaments sit around the predicted velocity maximum, which explains the flat dependence, although they move at a few percent of the velocity scale of the model. The amplitude discrepancy, the campaign-level calibration, and the radial-only geometry are the three areas in which a dedicated experiment or a released geometry model would improve on what the archive alone can give.
