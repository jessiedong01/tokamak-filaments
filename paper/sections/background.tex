\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Filament motion.}
A filament is a coherent field-aligned tube of plasma, with a radius $a$ of the order of a few ion gyroradii to a few centimetres \citep{krasheninnikov2001scrape} and with greater density and temperature than the surrounding scrape-off layer (SOL) plasma. Magnetic curvature and gradient drifts in the outboard SOL transverse to the filament cause ions and electrons to separate, producing a polarization electric field that drives a radial $E \times B$ drift that moves the entire filament radially outward \citep{krasheninnikov2001scrape, dippolito2011convective}. The drift velocity is determined by the potential that results from the charge separation, and is sensitive to how the polarization charge is removed through the dissipation pathways. This leads to two limiting cases. When charge drains through the field-aligned sheath to the divertor target, the filament drifts at a speed $v \propto c_s (L_\parallel/R)(\rho_s/a)^2$ \citep{krasheninnikov2001scrape}, where $c_s$ is the sound speed, $\rho_s$ is the ion sound gyroradius, $L_\parallel$ the connection length, and $R$ the major radius. When the circuit is completed through the ion polarization current within the filament \citep{garcia2006fluctuations}, the drift speed is instead $v \propto c_s (a/R)^{1/2}$.

\paragraph{The two-region model.}
\citet{myra2006collisionality} generalized these limits, coupling the midplane and divertor leg in a two-region model to find the characteristic scale for the filament radius, the characteristic velocity scale, and the dimensionless width and collisionality,
\begin{equation}
    a^* = \rho_s^{4/5} L_\parallel^{2/5} R^{-1/5}, \qquad
    v^* = c_s \left(\frac{2 a^*}{R}\right)^{1/2}, \qquad
    \Theta = \left(\frac{a}{a^*}\right)^{5/2}, \qquad
    \Lambda = \frac{\nu_{ei} L_\parallel}{\Omega_e \rho_s},
    \label{eq:tworegion}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_{ei}$ is the electron--ion collision frequency and $\Omega_e$ is the electron gyrofrequency \citep{dippolito2011convective}. Defining the dimensionless width $\hat a = a/a^*$ and velocity $\hat v = v/v^*$,
\begin{equation}
    \hat v =
    \begin{cases}
        \hat a^{1/2} & \text{connected ideal interchange } (\Lambda < 1,\ \Theta < 1), \\
        \hat a^{-2} & \text{sheath connected } (\Lambda < 1,\ \Theta > 1), \\
        \Lambda\, \hat a^{-2} & \text{resistive X-point } (1 < \Lambda < \Theta), \\
        \hat a^{1/2} & \text{resistive ballooning } (\Lambda > 1,\ \Lambda > \Theta).
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:regimes}
\end{equation}
At low collisionality, this predicts an increasing velocity with size as $\hat a^{1/2}$ for small filaments, a peak in the velocity around $\hat a = 1$, and a decreasing velocity with size as $\hat a^{-2}$ for large filaments. These limits are derived from scaling arguments, and more realistic simulations show slightly smaller velocities by factors of near unity due to details of filament profile and parallel structure \citep{easy2014three, walkden2015characterization, paruta2018blob}. Measurements of the velocity--size relation on the TORPEX basic plasma device \citep{theiler2009blob} found a transition between these predicted regimes.

\paragraph{Camera measurements.}
Visible imaging is sensitive to the line-integrated $D_\alpha$ emission, which depends on local density, temperature, and neutral density. Filaments show up as intense, field-aligned streaks riding on the background emission, and their emission is a close approximation of the filament density perturbation. Imaging-derived velocities and widths agree with probe measurements within tens of percent \citep{zweben2007edge, benayed2009interelm}. In the MAST tangential viewing geometry, the brightest feature in the camera image is the plasma limb, which is the region where the line of sight is tangent to the flux surfaces near the last closed flux surface (LCFS); filaments cross this limb as they move outward. Because the camera integrates over each exposure, a filament moving at velocity $v$ travels a distance $v\,t_{\rm exp}$ during the exposure, smearing the image of the filament over this distance in the direction of its motion (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
