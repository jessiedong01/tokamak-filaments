\section{Within-shot calibration}
\label{app:calibration}
The within-shot regression exploits the few centimetres of motion of the plasma during a flat top. For the \nWithinShot{} discharges in our pool in which the LCFS moves by at least 2~cm, we find that the median scale is \withinScaleMed~mm/pixel, with interquartile range \withinScaleQLo--\withinScaleQHi~mm/pixel, with extreme values of $< 1$~mm and $> 10^{\withinScaleMaxLog}$~mm. This large spread may be due to three factors. First, the EFIT boundary measurements are least reliable at the beginning and end of a flat top, at which we get most movement; second, as density increases throughout the flat top, the brightness profile is deformed, causing the peak at a fixed $R_{\rm LCFS}$ to move; finally, the lever arm is small, so a small error of 1~cm in $R_{\rm LCFS}$ causes a large error of tens of percent in our slope. The between-shot regression of Section~\ref{sec:calibration} is much more stable, as we consider the variation of position over a campaign, which is typically 10--20~cm, rather than the few centimetres of intra-discharge motion.
